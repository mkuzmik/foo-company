What Ravens Actually Remember

By Maren Ostby, Co-Founder & Creative Director · November 2025

We get asked a lot why a gear company is named after a bird, so here's the longer answer than what fits on the About page. It isn't a mascot we picked because it looked good on a hangtag — ravens are genuinely strange animals, and the more we read about them while naming our first pack, the more the name made sense.

They plan for things that haven't happened yet

Ravens have been observed setting aside tools they'll need later, sometimes for tasks that are minutes or hours away. That kind of forward planning used to be considered a mostly human trait. It's part of why our product names lean on Norse mythology's Huginn and Muninn — Odin's ravens, sent out to scout the world and report back — rather than just picking a bird that looked nice.

They hold a grudge, and a favor

Ravens are known to recognize individual human faces for years, and to treat people differently based on past interactions — friendlier toward people who've fed them, and noticeably wary of people who've threatened them, even long after the fact. Researchers studying wild populations have documented ravens altering their behavior around specific individuals well after a single encounter.

They solve problems in steps

Multi-step problem solving — the kind that requires doing something that doesn't pay off immediately, in order to set up something that does — shows up reliably in raven cognition studies. It's the same instinct behind caching food in dozens of hidden spots and remembering the map days later.

None of this is why we called it "Raven & Ridge" instead of something safer like "Trailhead Supply Co." But it's why the name stuck once we found it.

Why it shapes how we design

We didn't set out to make every product a raven pun. But once you spend enough time reading about an animal that plans ahead, remembers who helped it, and works a problem in stages rather than all at once, it's hard not to want your gear to behave the same way — built for the trip after this one, not just the trip you're on.
