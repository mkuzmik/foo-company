Field Dispatch: The 2026 Ambassador Cohort

By Kwame Asante, Field Test Director · February 2026

Every October we open applications for the next year's Ambassador Program, and every February we introduce the twelve people who'll spend the year field-testing gear before it ships, in exchange for a revenue share on anything they help design. Here's a first look at this year's cohort.

The guides

Three of this year's ambassadors lead guided trips professionally, which means their feedback tends to be blunt in the best way — they're testing gear against paying clients' expectations, not just their own tolerance for discomfort. Expect their notes to show up in next year's pack and boot updates first.

The thru-hikers

Five ambassadors this year are coming off long-distance thru-hikes, which puts thousands of miles on a small kit before they've even started testing for us. Their focus this cohort is weight and repairability — two things that only become obvious after month three on trail.

The photographers

The remaining four are photographers and trip journalists, chosen less for mileage and more for how long they're willing to sit still in bad weather waiting for a shot. Their gear notes tend to focus on things the rest of us undervalue: glove dexterity, pocket access, and how a shell holds up to being sat on a wet rock for an hour.

Applications for the 2027 cohort open this October. Watch the Journal and our socials for the announcement, or check the FAQ for eligibility details.

What happens next

Over the coming months this group will be carrying unreleased prototypes on their own trips, filing field notes back to the workshop, and in a few cases, getting a design credit and a cut of sales if something they pushed for actually ships. It's the same pipeline that got us the current hip belt on the Huginn 42 — a change one ambassador refused to stop complaining about until we fixed it.

Curious about applying, or want to nominate someone for next year's cohort? Email hello@ravenandridge.example.
