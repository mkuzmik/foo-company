How to Re-Seal a Rain Shell at Home

By Ingrid Solberg, Materials Lead · January 2026

Seam tape is the first thing to fail on any waterproof shell, usually years before the fabric itself gives out. The good news: reviving it is a twenty-minute job with a household iron, not a reason to retire the jacket. Here's the process we use on the repair bench, scaled down for a kitchen table.

What you'll need

  • A roll of seam-sealing tape rated for your shell's fabric (check the care label, or ask us — we can tell you which type your Corax shell uses)
  • A clothes iron with no steam function active
  • A thin cotton cloth or old pillowcase
  • Scissors and a clean, flat surface

1. Find the failure

Turn the jacket inside out and look along the taped seams, especially at the shoulders and underarms, for spots where the tape is peeling, bubbled, or has come away at the edges. Mark each spot with a small piece of masking tape on the outside so you don't lose track once you flip it back.

2. Trim the old tape

Carefully peel back any tape that's already lifting. If it's fully attached but just looks tired, leave it — you're patching failures, not stripping the whole seam.

3. Cut new tape to size

Cut a strip about an inch longer than the damaged section on each side, so the new tape overlaps healthy, still-bonded tape rather than butting up against another failure point.

4. Heat-set it

Set the iron to a medium, no-steam setting. Lay the cotton cloth over the new tape, and press — don't slide — for about 20 seconds per section, moving in overlapping passes along the whole strip. Let it cool fully before flexing the seam.

Never put an iron directly on shell fabric or tape. The cloth barrier isn't optional — a bare iron will melt some fabrics in seconds.

5. Test before you trust it

Run the seam under a light stream of water in the sink before your next trip. If it beads and sheds, you're done. If it soaks through immediately, the tape didn't bond — usually because the seam was damp or dusty before you started.

When to send it to us instead

If the fabric itself has delaminated, or the failure runs along more than a few inches of seam, that's past a home fix. That's exactly what the Gear Rebuild Program is for — mail it in and we'll re-tape it properly on the same machines we use for new production.
