Five Ridge Traverses Worth the Detour

By Kwame Asante, Field Test Director · March 2026

Every season our field team files reports from routes all over the map, and every season a handful of them get passed around the workshop until everyone's hiked them at least once. These are this year's five — ranked by nothing more scientific than how often we catch ourselves talking about them.

1. The Split Pine Route

2 hours · 4.5 miles · Easy scramble. A short out-and-back that gains most of its elevation in the first mile, then flattens into an open ridge walk with views back down into the valley. Good for testing a new pair of boots before committing to something longer.

2. Halfmoon Traverse

Half day · 9 miles · Moderate. Follows an old fire-lookout trail along a curved ridge that gives the route its name. Exposed in the middle third, so this is a good one to save for a clear-weather window rather than a chance-of-storms day.

3. Long Timber Ridge

Full day · 14 miles · Moderate to strenuous. Mostly forested until the final climb breaks out above the treeline for the last two miles. This is where we field-test most of our pack prototypes, since the trail surface changes constantly between roots, scree, and packed dirt.

Pack note: Long Timber Ridge is brutal on hip belts that don't breathe. It's part of why the Huginn 42's ventilated belt exists in the first place.

4. Widow's Saddle Loop

Overnight · 18 miles · Strenuous. A two-day loop with one exposed camp at the saddle itself, which means checking the forecast twice before you go. The reward is a sunrise that hits the whole ridge at once, if you time it right.

5. Corvid Hollow Divide

2–4 days · 31 miles · Strenuous. Our home traverse, starting a few miles from the workshop and following the ridgeline that gave the company its name. Every new hire does at least one leg of it in their first year, whether or not it's technically in their job description.

If you've done one of these and it didn't match our notes, tell us about it — trail conditions change, and this list gets revised more often than we publish it.

Spotted an error, or have a route worth adding? Email hello@ravenandridge.example — this list gets updated based on reader tips.
